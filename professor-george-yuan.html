My Proposed Research

Digital Finance and SME Credit Access: Examining Whether Digital Transaction Records Can Reduce Financing Constraints for Small Businesses in Sindh, Pakistan

My proposed research looks at a practical problem faced by small businesses in Sindh, Pakistan: access to formal credit can be difficult when businesses have limited financial records, limited credit history, insufficient collateral or information that banks can use for credit assessment.

At the same time, businesses are increasingly using digital financial services such as bank transactions, digital payments and other electronic financial activities. These activities can generate transaction records that may contain useful information about a business's financial behaviour.

The Problem I Want to Study

The main issue is not simply whether small businesses use digital finance. The more important question is whether the financial information generated through digital transactions can become useful for formal credit assessment.

Research problem: Small businesses may conduct regular economic activity but still have difficulty demonstrating their financial position to formal lenders because their traditional financial records and credit histories may be limited.

This creates an information problem between the business and the financial institution. A bank may have limited information about the business even when the business is actively receiving and making payments.

Research Gap

Existing research has increasingly examined digital finance, financial inclusion and technology-based credit assessment. There is also research on using alternative and big-data information to improve SME credit assessment.

However, my proposed study takes a different and more specific approach. Instead of focusing only on the development of a credit scoring model, I want to examine whether the availability and quality of digital financial records are associated with better access to formal credit among small businesses in a developing-country context.

The study will focus specifically on small businesses in Sindh, Pakistan, and will examine digital financial records as a possible source of information that can complement traditional credit assessment.

Research gap I want to investigate: There is a need for more evidence on how digital financial records generated by small businesses themselves may relate to financial information availability, information asymmetry and access to formal credit in the context of small businesses in Sindh, Pakistan.

Why My Research Aligns With Prof. Yuan's Research

The connection between my research and Prof. Yuan's work is mainly around the use of financial technology and additional information to improve the assessment of small businesses.

Digital Finance ↓ Digital Financial Records ↓ Better Financial Information ↓ Reduced Information Asymmetry ↓ Credit Assessment ↓ Formal SME Credit Access

Prof. Yuan's research examines FinTech and big-data approaches to SME credit rating and risk assessment. My proposed research starts from the same broad problem of limited information in SME financing, but approaches it from the perspective of digital financial records generated through normal business transactions.

I am particularly interested in understanding whether these records can provide useful information for credit assessment and whether businesses with more complete digital financial information face fewer financing constraints.

Specific Research Connection

One of Prof. Yuan's research directions involves using big-data and FinTech methods to assess SMEs when traditional accounting and financial information may be limited.

My proposed research is related to this problem but focuses on the source of information from a different angle: digital financial transaction records produced by the business.

This creates an opportunity to investigate whether these records can improve the information available for credit assessment before moving toward more advanced credit-scoring or machine-learning applications.

In simple terms: Prof. Yuan's work asks how technology and data can help evaluate SME credit risk. My research asks whether the digital financial records already being generated by small businesses can provide better information for that evaluation.

What I Plan to Examine

  1. Examine the use of digital financial services among small businesses in Sindh.
  2. Examine whether greater availability and quality of digital financial records are associated with access to formal credit.
  3. Examine whether digital financial information is associated with lower financing constraints.
  4. Examine the role of business size, business age, collateral, credit history and record-keeping practices.
  5. Explore whether digital financial information can complement traditional information used in SME credit assessment.

Main Research Question

To what extent can digital financial records reduce financing constraints and improve access to formal credit for small businesses in Sindh, Pakistan?

Supporting Questions

  • How widely are digital financial services used by small businesses in Sindh?
  • Is the availability of digital financial records associated with formal credit access?
  • Is better digital financial information associated with lower financing constraints?
  • Can digital financial information complement traditional credit assessment information?

Proposed Methodology

I currently plan to use a quantitative cross-sectional survey of small businesses in selected districts of Sindh, Pakistan.

The survey will examine digital finance usage, digital financial records, formal credit access, financing constraints and relevant business characteristics.

The initial analysis will include descriptive statistics, reliability analysis, correlation analysis and multiple regression. The final analytical model will be refined after reviewing the relevant literature and discussing the research design with a prospective supervisor.
